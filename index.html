About our maps

Schematic is drawn from the same coordinates as everything else and always renders. Terrain pulls OpenTopoMap tiles — real contours, relief and spot heights — but a published page runs in a sandbox that often blocks external map tiles. If it does, the button above and the per-day Google Maps links below still work, and saving this file and opening it from your desktop restores the terrain map.

Check before you drive

    The per-day figure is moving time on the routing shown. The clock times in each roadbook are not — they spread the whole day, departure to arrival, across the distance, so stops, food and toll queues are already in them. That slack runs from about a fifth of moving time on the motorway transfer up to double it on the easy last days, median around two thirds. You will still spend longer at Fuscher Törl than you plan to.

    Distances are measured with a routing engine through the waypoints listed, not estimated and not driven. They agree with Google to within about a per cent on the days spot-checked. Two things still move them: a waypoint marks where the road passes a town rather than the town centre, so stopping in one adds a little; and the router picks its own line between waypoints, which is the shortest way round, not necessarily the prettiest. Altitudes and the climbed totals remain approximate. The Google Maps link on each day is the authority — it knows today's roadworks, closures and diversions, and this page does not.

    Sunrise and sunset are calculated for each day's own start and end point, and for the day you are reading this: day 0 is today, day 1 tomorrow, and so on. Move the trip and the light moves with it, so check them again once the dates are fixed. Times are Central European, which covers all four countries here.
